[ 01 ] The thesis

Every request your team sends to an AI tool carries more than they meant to send. A cleaned-up email carries a client's name. A pasted stack trace carries a live key. A quick question to an agent carries the account number it needs to act. And whatever they type doesn't stay with them: it travels to a server you have no way to inspect, gets logged, and becomes someone else's problem to secure.

The usual answer is to watch the wire: a proxy in the cloud that reads the traffic, flags the violations, keeps the audit trail. But a service that has to read your sensitive data in order to protect it is just one more place that data now lives. That doesn't remove the exposure. It relocates it.

Olo's premise is narrow and absolute: the only data you can promise is private is the data that was never sent. So there is no our-cloud step. We classify and redact on the device, in the millisecond before the request exists on the wire. Nothing sensitive leaves, so there is nothing downstream to leak, log, subpoena, or breach.
